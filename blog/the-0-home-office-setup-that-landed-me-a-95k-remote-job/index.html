The $0 Home Office Setup That Landed Me a $95k Remote Job

Every remote work influencer will try to sell you a $3,000 desk setup. I got hired with a $0 one. Here's what actually matters.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 28 May 2026 5 min read

What My Home Office Actually Looked Like on Day of Hire

When I applied for my first $90k+ remote role, my home office was a kitchen table shared with my roommate, a five-year-old laptop with a cracked corner, and my phone propped against a stack of three books as a camera. Natural window light from the left. Total cost: $0.

I got the job.

What Hiring Managers Actually Care About

Here's the thing nobody tells you: hiring managers do not care about your desk setup. They care about whether you show up looking competent and engaged.

Competent and engaged looks like:

  • Eye-level camera — a stack of books, a box, anything that lifts your phone or laptop
  • Light facing you, not behind you — natural window light is free and better than most ring lights
  • A clean, uncluttered frame — even a plain wall
  • Wired internet on interview day — borrow a cable if you need to
  • No background noise — find a quiet 45 minutes, close the windows
That's it. That's the entire setup. The $3,000 standing desk, the ring light, the microphone arm — they're nice. They are not the reason someone gets hired.

The Real Cost of Waiting for the Perfect Setup

I have coached people with beautiful studio setups who couldn't land an interview. I have coached people using their phone propped on a shoebox who got three offers.

The setup doesn't close the deal. Your preparation does.

Every week someone says: "I'll start applying properly once I have a better setup." Every week the answer is the same: the setup is not the reason. The reason is always preparation, positioning, and practice. Never the desk.

Stop spending. Start preparing.
