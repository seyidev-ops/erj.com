The Cold Message That Gets Replies: Template, Plus Why Every Line Is There

Six sentences. No flattery, no life story, no attachment. Here is the message, and the reasoning behind each part so you can adapt it rather than copy it.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 13 July 2026 5 min read

Yesterday: how to find the person. Today: what to send them.

Almost every cold message fails for the same three reasons. It is too long, it is about the sender rather than the reader, and it asks for too much. Fix those three and the reply rate changes dramatically.

The template

Hi [Name],

I applied for the [Role] opening yesterday, so this is just a short note to say why I did.

I spent the last [X years] doing [the closest thing you have done], where I [one specific result with a number].

I noticed [one specific, true observation about their product, team or a recent change]. That is the kind of problem I like working on.

Happy to answer anything by message if it is useful. Either way, good luck with the search.

[Your name]

Why each line is there

"I applied for the [Role] opening yesterday." Immediately explains why a stranger is in their inbox. Removes the awkwardness in the first six words.

"so this is just a short note" Sets expectations. They now know this will be quick, which is why they keep reading.

The one specific result with a number. Not your whole history. One thing, concrete, checkable. A number makes it feel real in a way adjectives never do.

The specific observation. This is the line that separates you from everyone. It proves you looked. It can be small — a feature they shipped, a job listing detail, something they wrote. It must be true and specific. "I love your mission" is not an observation, it is filler.

"Happy to answer anything by message." The ask is almost nothing. You are not requesting a call, a coffee, or thirty minutes. Low cost to say yes.

"Either way, good luck with the search." Releases the pressure. Counter-intuitively this raises replies, because it reads as a person being decent rather than a candidate performing.

What to leave out

  • Your CV as an attachment. They can open your application.
  • Your full career history. Six sentences.
  • "I am a hard-working and passionate professional." Everyone says this and it carries no information.
  • Anything about needing the job. True, understandable, and it does not help you here.

Follow up once

Five working days later, one sentence in the same thread: "Just floating this back up in case it is useful — happy to be told it is not the right fit." Then stop. A second follow-up costs you more than it gains.

What to expect

Do not expect everyone to reply. A reasonable rate on ten well-researched messages is two or three responses, and one of those may be a polite no. That is a working system, not a failure.
