The Home Setup That Survives Power Cuts — And What Interviewers Ask About It

You do not need an expensive office. You need redundancy on three things. Here is the honest minimum, roughly what it costs, and what to say when you are asked.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 19 July 2026 5 min read

The setup question comes up in almost every interview for a role hired out of Africa. It is a fair question and it has a good answer, but only if you have actually built the thing.

The three things that must never fail

1. Internet, twice

One fibre or main connection, plus a mobile data backup on a different network. The backup matters more than the speed of the main one. A call at 12 megabits that never drops beats a call at 100 megabits that dies twice a week.

Set your laptop and phone up so switching takes seconds, not minutes. Practise it once so you are not learning during a client call.

2. Power, with a buffer

An inverter with enough battery for four to six hours of a laptop, router and lamp. That covers the ordinary outage. Solar on top if the budget allows, but it is not the entry requirement.

A laptop that holds a real charge matters as much as the inverter, and it is the thing people forget.

3. Audio

This is the one that gets underestimated. Interviewers forgive a slightly dark picture and do not forgive audio they have to strain through. Wired headphones with a microphone are enough. Bluetooth earbuds drop and stutter; wired ones do not.

The rest, in order of return

  • Light in front of you. A window or a cheap desk lamp facing you. Never a window behind you — it turns you into a silhouette.
  • A plain background. A wall. Virtual backgrounds glitch around your head and look worse than a real wall.
  • Camera at eye level. Books under the laptop. Costs nothing, changes how you look on a call completely.
  • A door, or an agreement. If a door is not possible, an agreement with the household about the hours you cannot be interrupted.

The fallback plan is part of the setup

Know the co-working space or reliable café nearest you, what it costs for a day, and how long it takes to get there. Having a named answer to "what if everything fails at once" is the difference between sounding prepared and sounding hopeful.

What to say when asked

"Two connections on separate networks, an inverter that covers about six hours, and a co-working space fifteen minutes away as a last resort. If something is going to affect a meeting I flag it early rather than disappearing. In the last year I have moved one meeting for this reason."

Concrete, unbothered, no defensiveness. It communicates that this is solved and not a topic that needs more airtime.

What you do not need

A dedicated room, a standing desk, two monitors, a ring light, a microphone on an arm. None of it will win you a role. Redundancy will.
