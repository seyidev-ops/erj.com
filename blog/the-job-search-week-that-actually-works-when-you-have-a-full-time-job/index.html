The Job Search Week That Actually Works When You Have a Full-Time Job

Six focused hours a week, spread so it survives a demanding job and a long commute. The exact blocks, and what to drop when the week collapses.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 29 July 2026 5 min read

Most advice assumes you are unemployed and have eight hours a day. Most people reading it are working full-time, commuting, and stealing time in the evenings. Here is the version built for that.

The six hours

Sunday evening, 90 minutes — sourcing. Build the week’s list of eight to ten roles. Check the location line, skim the requirements, save the links in one sheet. No applications tonight. This is the easiest block and it makes every other block possible.

Tuesday, 60 minutes — apply to three. Adjust the CV wording to each listing, apply through the portal, note it in the sheet.

Wednesday, 60 minutes — apply to three more. Same. Six applications, done properly, by midweek.

Thursday, 60 minutes — outreach. Find the hiring manager for the six roles you applied to and send the short message. Highest-return hour of your week.

Saturday morning, 90 minutes — follow-ups and building. Follow up on last week’s messages, then improve one thing: a case study, a CV bullet, your LinkedIn About section.

Six hours. Eight applications, six messages, one improvement, every week.

When the week collapses

It will. Deadline at work, a sick child, a funeral, no light for two days. When that happens, protect the blocks in this order:

  1. Thursday outreach. Never drop this one. It produces most of the replies.
  2. Sunday sourcing. Without a list, nothing else can happen.
  3. Applications — do three instead of six.
  4. Saturday building — drop it entirely for a week without guilt.

Half a week done is enormously better than a full week skipped, because the compounding comes from continuity rather than intensity.

The energy problem

The real obstacle is not the hours, it is doing this after a full day when you are tired and it feels pointless. Three things help.

Fixed slots, not "when I have time". Put them in your calendar with alarms. Decision-making at 9pm is unreliable; a calendar entry is not.

Lower the bar on bad days. One application is a day you did not break the chain. Zero is how streaks die.

Look at the sheet weekly. When nothing is happening externally, the record of what you have sent is the only visible evidence of progress. Read it deliberately.

How long to expect

At this pace, a realistic timeline to a first international offer is eight to sixteen weeks for someone with relevant skills and a properly rebuilt CV. Longer if you are changing field.

Anyone promising three weeks is selling something. Knowing the real number is what stops you quitting at week six, which is where most people stop — usually one or two weeks before the replies would have started.
