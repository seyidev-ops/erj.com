The Twenty Minutes After Someone Says Yes to a Call

Most people celebrate, then wait. The twenty minutes right after a recruiter agrees to a call decide more than the hour you spend preparing the night before.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 12 August 2026 6 min read

An email arrives agreeing to a call. What most people do next is read it four times, tell someone, and then do nothing until the evening before.

Those twenty minutes are the cheapest advantage in the whole process, because you are the only candidate who is awake to the conversation right now.

One: reply inside the hour, and propose specifics

Not "any time works for me". That sounds accommodating and it creates work for the person reading it. Offer three concrete slots with your own time zone stated and theirs converted: Tuesday 3pm WAT (10am ET), Wednesday 4pm WAT (11am ET), Thursday 2pm WAT (9am ET). You have just demonstrated the single competence they are most worried about — that you can operate across a time difference without making it their problem.

Two: write down who you are talking to, before you research them

Recruiter, hiring manager, or someone who will be your peer? They want completely different things. A recruiter is screening for eligibility, salary fit and obvious risk. A hiring manager is screening for whether you can do the work and whether managing you will be expensive. A peer is screening for whether you will make their week harder. The same answer lands differently in each room.

Three: pull the advert into a file and mark it up

While it is still live. Adverts get taken down, edited, or reposted with different wording, and then you are preparing for a job description you half remember. Copy it into a document with the date, then underline every requirement stated as a must, and write beside each one the specific piece of evidence you would give. Where you have no evidence, write that too — those are your weak points, and knowing them a week early is the difference between a fumble and a prepared answer.

Four: decide your number now, in the calm

Before anyone asks. The worst version of this conversation is the one where a recruiter asks for your expectation in minute six and you invent a figure under pressure, then live inside it for two years. Research the band, pick a range with a floor you will not go under, and write it down. Deciding in advance is most of the skill.

Five: send nothing else

Resist the follow-up that adds a portfolio link, a correction, a thought you had. It reads as anxiety and it moves you from candidate to project. You have replied well. Stop.

Why this is the Conversion point

Interviews do not pay you; a signed offer does. Most people work hardest on the parts of the search that feel like effort — sending more applications, rewriting the CV again — and treat the actual conversations as something that happens to them. The conversations are the only part where the money is decided.

If your applications produce interviews but not offers, your CV and your targeting are already working. That is genuinely good news. The leak is here, and it is the most fixable of the four.
