Time Zones Do Not Have to Stop You: How African Remote Workers Win the Overlap Game

The most common objection African remote workers face is time zone distance. Here is how to address it before it is raised — and turn it into an advantage.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 5 June 2026 5 min read

The Objection You Will Hear

"We need someone closer to our time zone." Most candidates accept the rejection. The best candidates address it before it is raised — in their headline, cover note, and first interview answer.

The WAT Overlap Reality

West Africa Time (UTC+1) gives you genuine overlap with most of the remote-hiring world:

  • UK and EU teams: Near-perfect overlap during standard business hours
  • US East Coast: 4–6 hours of overlap (9am–3pm WAT / 8am–2pm ET)
  • US West Coast: 2–4 hours of overlap — possible with an early start
  • Asia-Pacific: Limited, but async-first companies do not require real-time overlap

Address It Proactively in Your CV

State your overlap hours explicitly: "Based in Nigeria (WAT, UTC+1). Full overlap with UK/EU teams. 5-hour overlap with US East Coast. Async-first communication by default." Better still: name the tools. A candidate who mentions Loom, Notion, and structured EOD messages has already solved the time zone problem before the interview begins.

The Advantage Nobody Talks About

A WAT-based remote worker is the first person on the team to see overnight messages from Asia. They can brief the UK team at 9am with overnight updates already processed. That is not a liability — it is a coverage asset. Frame it that way.
