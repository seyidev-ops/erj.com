Twelve Small Mistakes That Quietly Kill Applications

None of these are dramatic. All of them are common, and each one removes you from consideration before anyone assesses your ability.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 1 August 2026 5 min read

These do not cause a rejection email. They cause silence, which is why people never learn they are making them. Run this list against your last five applications.

1. The CV file is named CV_final_v7.pdf

It sits in a folder with two hundred others. Name it Firstname_Lastname_Role_CV.pdf so a human can find it again.

2. The email address is undergraduate

bigboy_chidi@yahoo.com does not survive first contact. Firstname.lastname at gmail, made in five minutes.

3. Two-column CV design

It looks professional to you and turns to scrambled text inside the tracking software. One column.

4. Contact details in the header

Many systems do not read headers and footers. Your details are then simply absent. Put them in the body of the page.

5. A LinkedIn profile that does not match the CV

Different dates, different titles, different company spellings. Recruiters check, and mismatches read as carelessness at best.

6. Private LinkedIn profile

A recruiter searching for your skill cannot find you at all. This is the one that silently costs the most.

7. Applying to roles that exclude your country

"Remote, US only" means it. Read the location line first, every time.

8. The same CV for every role

The listing says Zendesk and HubSpot; your CV says "various CRM tools". The match fails. Use their words for the things you genuinely have.

9. A cover letter that is about you needing the job

"I am seeking an opportunity to grow" describes your need. They are solving their problem, not yours. Lead with what you can do for them.

10. No numbers anywhere

A CV of duties reads identically to everyone else in your role. One number per bullet changes it completely, and an honest estimate is fine.

11. A portfolio link that does not open

Permissions not set, or a PDF that never arrives. Test every link in a private browser window before sending. Nobody will email you to say it was broken.

12. Never following up

One short follow-up five working days later reaches people who genuinely meant to reply and got buried. It costs one sentence.

How to use this

Open your last five applications and check each one against the twelve. Most people find three or four. Fix them, and the same effort starts producing different results — not because you became a better candidate, but because you became a legible one.
