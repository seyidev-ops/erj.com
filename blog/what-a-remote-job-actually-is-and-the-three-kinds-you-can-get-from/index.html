What a Remote Job Actually Is (And the Three Kinds You Can Get From Nigeria)

"Remote job" is used to mean four different things, and picking the wrong one wastes months. Here are the three that are genuinely open to you from here, and what each pays.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 5 July 2026 5 min read

Most people start applying before they know what they are applying for. Then they spend four months chasing a category of work that was never open to them. Let us fix that in five minutes.

The word means four different things

When a company writes "remote", they mean one of these:

  • Remote (anywhere) — they hire from any country. This is what you want.
  • Remote (country-restricted) — "Remote, US only" or "Remote, EU". You will be filtered out no matter how good you are.
  • Hybrid — office two or three days a week. Not remote.
  • Remote-first with hubs — mostly remote, occasional travel. Sometimes open to you.

Read the location line before you read anything else. If it says a country you are not in, close the tab. That single habit will save you hours every week.

The three that are genuinely open to you

1. Full-time employment through an employer-of-record

The company hires you properly, but a service like Deel or Remote.com handles the paperwork because they have no legal entity in Nigeria. You get a monthly salary, usually paid in dollars.

Typical range in 2026: $800 to $2,500 a month for a first international role, depending on the function.

2. Long-term contract work

You invoice monthly. No paid leave, no health cover, but the rate is often higher and the hiring process is faster because there is less legal friction.

Typical range: $1,000 to $3,000 a month for steady, full-time-equivalent contract work.

3. Agency or outsourced roles

You work for a company that places you with international clients. Lower pay than going direct, but the easiest door for a first role, and the reference afterwards is real.

Typical range: $500 to $1,200 a month.

Which functions actually hire this way

Customer support, virtual assistance, bookkeeping, social media and content, sales development, quality assurance, data entry and annotation, design, and software development. If your current work sits near any of those, you have a route.

Notice what is not on that list: anything requiring you to physically touch something, and anything requiring a local licence.

What to do today

  1. Decide which of the three doors fits your situation. If you need income within eight weeks, door three is the fastest.
  2. Write down the two functions closest to what you already do.
  3. Open one job board and read ten listings in those functions. Do not apply. Just read the location line and the requirements, and notice the pattern.

You now know more than most people who have been applying for six months.
