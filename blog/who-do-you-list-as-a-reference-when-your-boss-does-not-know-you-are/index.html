Who Do You List as a Reference When Your Boss Does Not Know You Are Leaving?

The problem nobody warns you about, arriving right at the end of a process you have almost won. Here is how to handle it without burning anything.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 14 June 2026 5 min read

You get to the final stage, they ask for two references, and the only person who can properly speak to your work is the manager you have not told. This stops people at the finish line more often than it should.

First: you are allowed to say no, for now

The standard, entirely professional response:

"Happy to provide references — could we do that at the offer stage? My current employer does not yet know I am exploring, and I would rather tell them myself once something is confirmed."

No reasonable employer objects. Most expect it. Anyone who insists on contacting your current manager before an offer exists is telling you something useful about how they will treat you later.

Who to list instead

  • A former manager. The cleanest option. Someone who managed you at a previous employer has no conflict at all.
  • A colleague who has since left. They know your work and no longer report into your current chain.
  • A client or supplier. Especially strong for anyone in service or operations — an external voice is often more credible than an internal one.
  • A senior colleague who already knows. Only if you genuinely trust them. This is a real risk and you should weigh it honestly.
  • A lecturer, mentor, or someone you volunteered under. For a first role, entirely acceptable.

How to ask

Ask before you list anyone. Being contacted as a surprise reference produces lukewarm answers.

"Hi [Name], I am in the final stage for a remote [role] with [company]. Would you be willing to act as a reference? If yes, the things they will likely ask about are [two specific areas]. And if it is easier to decline, please just say so."

Then send them the job description. A reference who knows what the role needs gives a far more useful answer than one recalling you in general terms.

What to do at the offer stage

Once the offer is written and you have accepted, tell your manager before the reference request arrives. Being asked about someone who has not mentioned they are leaving is the version that damages the relationship.

Give proper notice, offer a real handover, and write down what only you know. You are likely to need this person in five years, and the remote world is smaller than it looks.

One thing to avoid

Do not list someone as a reference without telling them, and do not list a friend pretending to be a manager. Verification is routine, and a fabricated reference ends offers that were otherwise signed.
