"Why Do You Want to Work Remotely?" — The Answer That Loses Offers

It sounds like small talk. It is not. Most people answer it in a way that quietly tells the employer they are hiring a flight risk.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 11 June 2026 5 min read

This question comes early, it sounds friendly, and it is one of the few places where a candidate can talk themselves out of a role in a single sentence.

The answers that cost you

"I want the flexibility." The employer hears: this person wants to work when they feel like it. Flexibility is a real benefit and it is a terrible headline answer, because it is entirely about what you get.

"I want to avoid the commute." True for everyone. It says nothing about the work and reads as a complaint about your last job.

"I want to earn in dollars." Honest, understandable, and the wrong room for it. It tells them your motivation is the currency, not the job — which means a better rate elsewhere takes you tomorrow.

"I want to travel." Now they are worried about your time zone, your internet, and whether you will be in three countries by Q3.

What they are actually asking

Two things. Will this person still be here in eighteen months? And do they understand that remote work is harder in some ways, not easier?

A good answer proves you have thought about the second one. That is what separates you from the person who thinks remote means comfortable.

The structure that works

One line on how you work best — tied to output, not comfort. One line proving you know the difficulty — name something genuinely hard about remote work. One line on why this company — specific, not flattery.

A worked example

"I do my best work in long uninterrupted blocks, and an office day gets broken up in a way that costs me most of that. Remote gives it back — but I am clear that it costs something too. Nobody sees you working, so you get judged entirely on what you finish and how clearly you write, and you have to build your own structure because none is provided.

The reason it is this role specifically is that you are already distributed across four countries, so the writing discipline is a requirement rather than an aspiration. That is the environment I want to be good in."

Why that lands

It never mentions comfort, commute or currency. It shows you know the trade-off. And it ends on their company rather than on your preferences — which is the reversal that makes the whole answer feel like it was about them all along.
