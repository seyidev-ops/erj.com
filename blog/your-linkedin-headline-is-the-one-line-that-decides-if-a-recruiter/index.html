Your LinkedIn Headline Is the One Line That Decides If a Recruiter Clicks

It appears in every search result, every comment, every message. Most people waste it on their job title. Here is the formula and four worked examples.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 9 July 2026 4 min read

Your headline is the line under your name. It follows you everywhere on LinkedIn — search results, comments, connection requests, messages. It is the single most-seen sentence you own, and by default it just repeats your job title.

A job title tells someone what you are called. A headline should tell them what you do and who you do it for.

The formula

What you do + who you do it for + the result + remote availability

You do not need all four in every case, but the more you include the more searchable and more convincing you become.

Four worked examples

Before: Customer Service Officer at First Bank
After: Customer Support Specialist | Helping SaaS teams cut response times and keep customers | Open to remote roles

Before: Graphic Designer
After: Brand and Product Designer | I turn early-stage ideas into interfaces people can use | Remote, available now

Before: Accountant
After: Bookkeeper for small international teams | QuickBooks and Xero | Month-end close without the panic

Before: Looking for opportunities
After: Virtual Assistant for founders | Inbox, calendar and travel handled | 4 years supporting remote teams

Notice the last one. "Looking for opportunities" is the most common headline among people who are not getting found, and it is the weakest possible use of the space. It describes your need, not your value.

Why the keywords matter

Recruiters search LinkedIn the way you search Google. They type "customer support Zendesk remote" and get a list. If those words are not in your headline or profile, you are not in the list. It is that mechanical.

So put the words a recruiter would type. Your actual job title, the tools you use, and the word remote.

Three more things in the same ten minutes

  • Photo. Plain background, good light, your face filling most of the frame, no sunglasses. Profiles with a clear photo get looked at far more often.
  • Location. Set your real city. Then turn on "Open to work" and select Remote as a location type — that is what puts you in the remote filter.
  • Public profile. Settings, visibility, make sure your profile is visible to everyone. A private profile cannot be found by anyone who might hire you.

Ten minutes. Do it before you send your next application, so the recruiter who checks you actually finds something.
